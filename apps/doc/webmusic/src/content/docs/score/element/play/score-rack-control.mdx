`<score-rack-control>` is the mixing desk for a [`Rack`](/score/headless/play/rack/): a fader per member,
a master over them, and mute and solo on each. Write the parts inside it and wrap the whole thing in a
player; the demo mixes three parts of Arabesque No. 1 this way.

A [`<score-rack-part>`](#score-rack-part) is a **declaration**, not a player: it renders nothing and owns no
engine. A rack member is headless — a score and a sound, and the rack builds the `ScorePlayer` that
plays it — and this is how one is written down without pretending otherwise.

The desk announces its Rack to the containing player. Its direct `<score-rack-part>`
children supply member declarations; script can also add members to an assigned
Rack. The Rack owns the member players and their musical state.

## Import

```html
<script type="module">
  import {
    defineScorePlayerElement,
    defineRackControlElement,
    defineRackPartElement,
  } from '@webmusic/score/play/element';
  defineScorePlayerElement();
  defineRackControlElement();
  defineRackPartElement();
</script>

<score-player>
  <score-rack-control>
    <score-rack-part id="lead" src="/midi/Arabesque%20No.1.mid" sound="triangle"></score-rack-part>
  </score-rack-control>
</score-player>
```

## Ports and composition

`<score-rack-control>` is a **Rack**. Without an assigned `.rack` it builds a
`ScoreRackModule` in the enclosing scope, exposes it as `.module` and provides
that Rack's context to its children. Each direct `<score-rack-part>` becomes one
`ScorePlayerModule` (its id is the part `id`), admitted into one channel; the
part's `.module` borrows it and lists the same ports as a
[player's module](/score/element/play/score-player/#ports-and-composition). The
desk's own ports:

| Port | Family | Direction | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `params` | `control` | input | The `master-volume` parameter |
| `master` | `audio` | output | Master insert output, once prepared; routed to the enclosing destination |
| `channel:<id>` | `audio` | output | Post-insert tap of one admitted channel |

Inside a `<score-player>` the desk passes that player's Session through, so
parts join it rather than choosing a child as master. An outermost desk, or one
below a provider without a Session, owns a Session of its own:
`sessionTransport` returns it, and its commands start, pause and seek every
part together. Nesting another bus admits that one bus output and does not
duplicate descendant registrations.

Containment places a direct `<score-effect>` or `audio-effect` on the master
chain. Inside a `<score-rack-part>`, an effect belongs to that part's
post-fader channel inserts unless it sets `slot="master"`, and a view or
analysis tool borrows that part's playback. Elsewhere, `input="#lead"` names a
part for a companion, and an audio analyser can read the bus with
`input="#desk" port="master"`. Assigning a Headless `Rack` to `.rack` backs the
same mixer surface with that borrowed Rack instead and provides no module
context: declarations become its members and an enclosing player drives it. See
[the mixed example](/bridge/modular-composition/).

```html
<score-player>
  <score-rack-control id="desk">
    <score-rack-part id="lead" src="/midi/Arabesque%20No.1.mid" sound="triangle">
      <score-effect type="delay" time="0.25" wet="0.2"></score-effect>
      <score-view type="piano-roll"></score-view>
    </score-rack-part>
    <score-rack-part id="pad" src="/midi/Arabesque%20No.1.mid" sound="sine"></score-rack-part>
  </score-rack-control>
</score-player>
```

## What the desk shows

Its attributes are about what the desk **shows**. `master`, `mute` and `solo` each add or take away a
control, which is the only thing a mixing surface has to configure that is not already a property of
the rack behind it. Hiding mute or solo does not grey a button out: the command is not offered to the
mixer at all.

The rack is the single source of truth. Levels, mute and solo are read back off it rather than
mirrored here, so a fader cannot drift from the gain it moves — and because `Rack` reports a mix
change as well as a membership change, anything else driving the rack repaints this desk too.

A desk without resolved members shows the shared outlined-square waiting animation.
While a child `score-rack-part` loads its score, the desk shows the four-square loading
animation; existing channel controls remain available. A failed child load displays its
error, and replacement or cancellation cannot leave stale loading feedback. The declaration
itself stays nonvisual. Both animations use the shared grayscale status tokens and stop
for reduced motion, with descriptions retained for assistive technology.

## Master effects

Effects on the desk use the same recipes as
[`<score-player>`](/score/element/play/score-player/#add-an-effect-chain). Assign an
`Effect.chain(...)` to the desk's `.effect` property: its order is signal order, from the rack's
summed output through each effect, and it processes the master bus once rather than creating a
separate copy for every part.

## `<score-rack-part>`

One member of the desk, declared in markup. This section owns its nonvisual
contract. Outside a direct `<score-rack-control>` parent, `src` is not fetched and no
Rack member or playback engine is created.

| Member | Kind | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | attribute | `part-N` | Member id and fader label; `N` is the declaration's position among the desk's direct children. Changing it reconciles the member without reloading its score. |
| `src` | attribute | — | URL of the score this part contributes, loaded through the sibling `@webmusic/score/io` capability while inside a desk. |
| `format` | attribute | adaptive | Forces the parser when the extension is missing or lies. Only read while `src` is loading. |
| `sound` | attribute | the rack's default | Oscillator name: `sine`, `square`, `sawtooth` or `triangle`. |
| `.score` | property | — | A `Score` assigned directly. Wins over `src`. |
| `.sound` | property | — | A `HeadlessSynth` assigned directly. Wins over `sound`, and the only way to give a part a synth the attribute cannot name. |
| `webscore:error` | event | — | `{ operation: 'rack-part', error }`, bubbling and composed. |

The `id` names the fader the desk draws, so give each part one; a part with no `id` falls back to its
position among its siblings. There is no `rate`, `loop`, `pan` or chrome: a part is not a player, so
there is nothing on it for those to act on. The player wrapping the desk owns playback for every part
at once, and the desk owns the levels.

Changing `id` renames the live member without downloading its score again, rebuilding its player or
resetting its mix state. Duplicate requested ids leave existing members intact and emit an error.
Changing `sound` re-voices the part in place. Changing `src` or `format`
reloads its score, which does replace the member — `Rack.add` on an existing id disposes the live
player, and a new score is a different member.

A source replacement or disconnection aborts its pending request. Late results cannot replace
the current declaration. A failed current load emits `webscore:error`; the already resolved
declaration remains until replacement succeeds or its source is cleared. Removing a declaration
removes its member from the desk. Supplied sounds remain caller-owned.

## Filling the rack

`<score-rack-part>` covers the markup case. From script, build the members headless and hand a rack over
instead. Either assign one — adopted, and never disposed
here — or let the element create its own and reach it through `.rack`:

```ts
import { Rack, Sound } from '@webmusic/score/play/headless';
import type { RackControlElement } from '@webmusic/score/play/element';

const rack = new Rack();
rack.add({ id: 'lead', score: lead, sound: Sound.oscillator() });
rack.add({ id: 'bass', score: bass, sound: Sound.oscillator({ type: 'sawtooth' }) });

document.querySelector<RackControlElement>('score-rack-control')!.rack = rack;
```

Add or remove a member later and the desk redraws its strips; move a level from anywhere and the
faders follow.

<details class="component-section">
<summary>API</summary>


The desk reads master and member state from its Rack. Membership changes update
the strips; mix changes update their values. Direct `<score-rack-part>` children
declare members, and `.rack` can supply an existing Rack. Transport remains on
`<score-player>` or the application-owned Headless controller.

| Member | Kind | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `master` | attribute | Show the master strip. Absent means shown; `master="false"` leaves the channel strips alone. |
| `mute` | attribute | Show per-channel mute, enabled by default; `mute="false"` removes the command from the mixer. |
| `solo` | attribute | Show per-channel solo, enabled by default. Soloing one channel clears the rest; `solo="false"` removes the command. |
| `preload` | attribute | An outermost desk, which owns its Session, prepares every part as it joins: the AudioContext (created suspended; the first play resumes it), each part's engine, its channel and the inserts on it, so faders and effect panels are live before the first play. On by default; `preload="none"` defers all of it to the first play. A desk inside a player follows that player's `preload`. |
| child `<score-rack-part>` | markup | Each declares one rack member. Labelled by its `id`, or by position when it has none. |
| `.rack` | property | The `Rack` to mix. Assigning one adopts it — it is never disposed here. Left unset, the element creates its own and disposes it on disconnect. |
| `.sessionTransport` | property | Read-only `ModuleSession` the parts play in: the desk's own when it is outermost, otherwise the enclosing provider's; `undefined` while disconnected or when a Headless `.rack` is assigned. |
| `.effect` | property | Post-processing recipe for the rack's summed output. Assign an `Effect` or `Effect.chain(...)`; replacing it updates the owned or assigned Rack in place without rebuilding members. |
| `webscore:error` | event | `{ operation: 'rack-control', error }`, bubbling and composed. Reports member reconciliation, Rack mix commands, master-effect replacement, owned Rack cleanup or presenter failures. |

</details>

<details class="component-section">
<summary>Styling</summary>

The host fits its container and respects native `hidden`. The master stays beside
an internally scrollable channel bank whose scrollbar is hidden; strips are separated by
hairlines, names are uppercase mono captions that wrap within their strips, and a
nonempty bank can receive keyboard focus for horizontal scrolling. Resizing does
not rebuild controls or alter the Rack.

The `--rc-*` aliases override this desk only. Without them, controls keep their
`--wm-mixer-*` and shared UIKit theme inputs, including `--wm-foreground`,
`--wm-foreground-muted`, `--wm-surface-muted` and `--wm-focus-ring`. The fader fill falls back
to `--rc-fill` → `--wm-mixer-fill` → `--wm-foreground-muted`, and labels to `--rc-text` →
`--wm-mixer-text` → `--wm-foreground-muted`. Use the exposed parts to
change the strip or label layout without replacing the fader behavior.

The outer surface also reads `--wm-component-background`, `--wm-component-border`,
`--wm-component-radius` and `--wm-component-padding`. Override just this desk
with `--wm-rack-control-surface-background`, `--wm-rack-control-surface-border`,
`--wm-rack-control-surface-radius` or `--wm-rack-control-surface-padding`. Padding falls back
through `--wm-mixer-surface-padding`, `--wm-component-padding` and `--wm-mixer-padding` to `0`,
so the desk's rows run edge to edge and each strip carries its own inset.
Background accepts `transparent`; the surface border accepts a complete border
or `none`. Channel declarations (`score-rack-part`) have no visual surface.


```css
score-rack-control {
  --rc-border: light-dark(#d8d8d8, #333);
  --rc-fader-width: 2rem;
  --rc-fader-height: 96px;
  --rc-track: light-dark(#f3f3f3, #1c1c1c);
  --rc-track-border: light-dark(#d8d8d8, #333); /* soft control outline */
  --rc-fill: light-dark(#777, #aaa);
  --rc-thumb: light-dark(#111, #eee);           /* position indicator */
  --rc-radius: 0;
}
```
